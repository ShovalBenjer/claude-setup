**`strand.py`** enforces two rules over `docs/analysis/`, `docs/specs/`, `docs/prd/`:
every spec and PRD declares a status from a fixed vocabulary, and every governed document
is referenced by something that is not `docs/INDEX.md`. The index is excluded deliberately:
it links everything by construction, so counting it makes the rule vacuous.

First real run found **10 structure violations**, six specs with no status and four with a
status outside the vocabulary. All ten fixed, each status chosen from the document's own
content. It found **zero reachability strandings across 59 governed documents**, and that
zero is a finding rather than a pass: a hand pass the same day judged 7 of 37 analysis
documents stranded by the stronger test, whether their findings ever reached a mechanism.
**Link-counting cannot see a document that is linked and ignored.** That limit is in the
tool's docstring and printed in its own report.

The oracle corrected itself twice during construction. Its first parser rejected
`Status: superseded by <file>` and `Status: APPROVED for autonomous execution` as unknown
statuses. Both are legitimate forms the corpus uses. Rejecting a valid form is the oracle
checking its own ruled shape instead of the property, which is `L-2026-07-31-b`, and both
cases now have pinning tests.

**`atlas.py`** computes the corpus structure: cluster, status, size, date, inbound
references, and near-duplicate overlap by Jaccard over hashed five-word shingles. Published
view of the result is an artifact, not a document. It found **420 documents, 16 clusters,
30 near-duplicate pairs, of which 14 are a live file against its own dated archive.** Of
the 16 remaining, 11 are prompt version series and one is a generated-from relationship.
**Four are real merge candidates.** The corpus is far less duplicated than it feels; what
was missing was a way to look at it.

## 5. The external audit, both rounds, and what it changed

Codex under ChatGPT auth, sealed read-only bundle, criteria split into named questions.

**Round one: 8 findings, 1 critical.** The critical one was mine to eat. The spec proposed
summing per-lens log-likelihood ratios into a Wald boundary. **Mathematically invalid
here**: no lens has a calibrated sensitivity, the lenses inspect the same work product and
`mutate.py` audits the other three so they are not independent, and selection is adaptive.
Summing marginal ratios over correlated evidence double-counts it and forfeits the error
guarantee that was the entire reason to use Wald. Demoted to a two-phase design where
Phase A is fixed-N heterogeneous lenses and Phase B is gated on three measurements that do
not exist.

**Round two on the corrected documents: 7 more findings, 2 high.** Two were introduced by
round one's own fix. Phase B's boundary was to come from BOUNDARY's DDM fit, which fits
**agent task-completion stops**, a different decision with a different cost structure:
a category error dressed as reuse. And the 100-turn human holdout was called ground truth
when a human rater cannot observe the counterfactual either.

**Fifteen findings across two rounds. Six were problems my own corrections created.** The
design got smaller and narrower every time somebody checked it, which is the correct
direction of travel.
