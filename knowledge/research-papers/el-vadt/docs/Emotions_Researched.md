### **Concept 1: JOY**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Joy (established in broaden-and-build theory)[^13][^14]
- **Synonyms**: Happiness (though distinct as composite construct), elation, delight[^13]


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: Joy is a positive emotion characterized by high arousal and positive valence, resulting from appraisal of goal congruence or unexpected positive outcomes.[^15][^16][^17]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Appraisal**: Events appraised as matching goals, often unexpectedly[^18][^16]
- **Broaden-and-build function**: Joy broadens attention (~7.5 score vs. anger ~3 on broadening scale) and builds psychological resources (resilience)[^14][^13]
- **Neural distinction from gratitude**: Joy activates reward regions (putamen, perigenual ACC, vmPFC) primarily, while gratitude additionally recruits mentalizing regions (precuneus, TPJ) for benefactor-intention appraisal[^16]

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Not identical to happiness**: Happiness = composite (life satisfaction + coping resources + positive emotions)[^13]
- **Liking vs. wanting**: Berridge's framework distinguishes hedonic impact ("liking") from motivation ("wanting"); joy involves both but dissociable neurally[^19]

**Citations**:

- Fredrickson \& Cohn (2009, 2955 citations): Positive emotions (joy, contentment) predict resilience increase → life satisfaction[^13]
- Watkins (2020) review: Joy definitions disjointed in psychology; calls for integration[^20]
- Neural study (Yu et al.): Benefactor-intention (TPJ, precuneus) + benefit-value (putamen, pgACC/vmPFC) distinguish gratitude from joy[^16]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) Circumplex (Valence-Arousal) Model**[^3][^4][^21]

Joy = **high positive valence** + **moderate-to-high arousal**

$$
\text{Emotion}(t) = \begin{pmatrix} V(t) \\ A(t) \end{pmatrix}, \quad V_{\text{joy}} \in [+2, +4], \; A_{\text{joy}} \in [+1, +3]
$$

On 9×9 Affective Grid (Russell's model): Joy ≈ (V=+3, A=+2).[^4][^21]

**b) Broaden-and-Build Formal Model**[^14][^13]

Let $R(t)$ = resilience at time $t$, $PE(t)$ = positive emotions, $LS(t)$ = life satisfaction.

Empirical path model (Fredrickson \& Cohn 2009, N=86, 28 days):[^13]

$$
\begin{align}
\Delta R &= \beta_1 \cdot PE + \epsilon_1, \quad \beta_1 = 0.15, \; p = 0.03 \\
\Delta LS &= \beta_2 \cdot \Delta R + \beta_3 \cdot PE + \epsilon_2
\end{align}
$$

**Mediation confirmed**: $PE \rightarrow \Delta R \rightarrow \Delta LS$ (resilience mediates positive emotion → life satisfaction).

**c) Bayesian Appraisal Model**

Goal congruence appraisal triggers joy. Let $G$ = goal state, $O$ = observed outcome.

$$
P(\text{Joy} \mid O) \propto P(O \mid G_{\text{match}}) \cdot P(G_{\text{match}}) \cdot \text{Unexpectedness}(O)
$$

Where $P(O \mid G_{\text{match}})$ = likelihood outcome matches goal; unexpectedness amplifies intensity.[^2][^18]

**Assumptions**: Goal-congruence necessary; appraisal precedes emotion; broaden-and-build predicts downstream resilience building.

**Model Predictions**:

1. Joy broadens attention (measured via thought-action repertoire tasks)[^14]
2. Repeated joy experiences accumulate resilience[^13]
3. Joy induction via goal-congruent events should activate reward networks (putamen, vmPFC)[^16]

### **Concept 2: CONTENTMENT / SATISFACTION**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Contentment (established in broaden-and-build, recent 2025 research)[^33][^14]
- **Synonyms**: Satisfaction, serenity, calm, peace


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: Contentment is a **low-arousal positive emotion** characterized by a sense of sufficiency ("enoughness"), calmness, acceptance of the present moment, and absence of striving.[^33]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Appraisal**: Present situation appraised as adequate; no discrepancy between desired and actual states[^33]
- **Distinct from happiness**: Contentment is NOT a weaker version of happiness; it is qualitatively distinct, emphasizing stability over intensity[^33]
- **Broaden-and-build function**: Like joy, contentment broadens attention (~6 score) but is more sustainable because it doesn't depend on external highs[^14][^33]
- **Self-acceptance link**: Contentment strongly correlates with unconditional self-acceptance (stable, not performance-dependent)[^33]

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Low arousal differentiates from joy**: Joy = high arousal; contentment = low arousal[^21][^33]
- **Not apathy**: Contentment involves positive valence; boredom/indifference are neutral or negative

**Citations**:

- 2025 review (Psychology Today): "A Different Kind of Happiness: The Quiet Power of Contentment" — contentment as distinct low-arousal positive emotion[^33]
- Fredrickson's broaden-and-build: Contentment/serenity broadens thought-action repertoires (~6 score)[^14]
- Circumplex model: Contentment located in high V, low A quadrant[^21]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) VAD Model**[^24][^4][^21]

$$
\text{Contentment} = \begin{pmatrix} V \\ A \\ D \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} +2.5 \text{ to } +4 \\ -2 \text{ to } 0 \\ +1 \text{ to } +3 \end{pmatrix}
$$

High valence, low arousal, moderate-to-high dominance (sense of control/sufficiency).

**b) Free Energy Minimization (Bayesian)**[^34][^35]

Contentment as low prediction error state:

Let $F$ = free energy, $s$ = state, $o$ = observation.

$$
F = \mathbb{E}_{q(s)}[\log q(s) - \log p(s, o)] = D_{KL}(q \| p) + \text{Surprise}
$$

**Contentment hypothesis**: When $F$ is minimized and stable (low $\frac{dF}{dt}$), valence is positive and arousal is low. Contentment = homeostatic equilibrium in cognitive resource utilization.[^36][^34]

**c) Homeostatic Set-Point Model** (Boredom-related, by analogy)[^36]

Trudel et al. (2025) propose boredom as deviation from cognitive homeostatic set-point. Contentment is the INVERSE: residing AT the optimal cognitive engagement set-point.

$$
\text{Contentment} \propto -|\text{Cognitive Load} - \text{Optimal Load}|
$$

When cognitive load matches optimal engagement, contentment emerges. Both under- and over-stimulation reduce contentment.

**Assumptions**: Valence inversely related to free energy change; arousal reflects prediction error magnitude; contentment = stable, low-error state.

**Model Predictions**:

1. Contentment increases with self-acceptance (verified)[^33]
2. Contentment more sustainable than high-arousal joy (speculative, supported by broaden-and-build)[^33]
3. Neural: Reduced DMN activity (less self-oriented rumination, more present-moment), reward system moderate activation (hedonic but not intense)

### **Concept 3: ANGER**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Anger (established in appraisal theory)[^39][^2]
- **Synonyms**: Rage, fury, irritation (intensity variants), hostility (trait)


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: Anger is a negative emotion arising from appraisal of **goal incongruence** + **high certainty** + **high control** + **individual responsibility** (blame).[^40][^17][^39]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Appraisal dimensions**:[^40][^39]
    - **Goal obstruction**: Someone/something blocks goal attainment
    - **High certainty**: Clear understanding of situation (vs. fear's uncertainty)
    - **High control**: Perception one can influence/change situation
    - **Individual agency**: Other person responsible (vs. situational attribution in sadness)
- **Action tendency**: Approach, overcome obstacle, confront[^41][^40]
- **Functional role**: Goal-directed emotion; anger → greater goal-consistent decision-making vs. sadness/fear[^40]

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Anger ≠ sadness**: Sadness = goal loss (irreversible), situational control; anger = goal blocked (surmountable), individual control[^41][^39]
- **Anger ≠ fear**: Fear = low certainty, low control; anger = high certainty, high control[^39][^40]
- **Developmental**: Young children (36 months) show anger ↔ approach behavior; sadness ↔ withdrawal[^41]

**Citations**:

- Lerner \& Keltner (2001, 1535 citations): Anger increases perceptions of control and certainty; appraisal tendencies shape judgment/decision-making[^39]
- Appraisal theory (Moors 2020): Goal incongruence → anger; controllability dimension critical[^17][^2]
- Functional study (Har-Arad et al. 2024): Anger → goal-directed decision-making, less susceptible to contextual biases[^40]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) Appraisal Vector Model**[^42][^2][^39]

Let **A** = appraisal vector:

$$
\mathbf{A}_{\text{anger}} = \begin{pmatrix} \text{Goal relevance} \\ \text{Goal incongruence} \\ \text{Certainty} \\ \text{Control} \\ \text{Agency} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \text{High} \\ \text{High} \\ \text{High} \\ \text{High} \\ \text{Individual} \end{pmatrix}
$$

Emotion classification:

$$
P(\text{Anger} \mid \mathbf{A}) \propto \prod_{i} P(A_i \mid \text{Anger}) \cdot P(\text{Anger})
$$

Empirical support: Lerner \& Keltner showed anger ↔ high certainty + control appraisals in judgment tasks.[^39]

**b) Valence-Arousal Model**[^3][^4]

$$
\text{Anger} = (V_{\text{anger}}, A_{\text{anger}}) \approx (-2 \text{ to } -4, +2 \text{ to } +4)
$$

Negative valence, high arousal (similar to fear but distinguished by appraisal context).

**c) Dynamical Systems Model** (Emotion-Action Link)[^43][^41]

Let $I_{\text{anger}}(t)$ = anger intensity, $B_{\text{approach}}(t)$ = approach behavior.

$$
\frac{dB_{\text{approach}}}{dt} = \eta \left[ \alpha \cdot I_{\text{anger}}(t) - B_{\text{approach}}(t) \right]
$$

Where $\alpha > 0$: anger drives approach. Empirical fit (Cole et al. 2017, 85 citations): Within-person, increased anger ↔ increased approach in 36-month-olds (Locked Box task).[^41]

**Assumptions**: Appraisal precedes emotion; action tendencies follow emotion; control/certainty appraisals distinguish anger from other negative emotions.

**Model Predictions**:

1. Anger → approach behavior, not withdrawal[^41]
2. Anger → risk-seeking judgments (high certainty/control reduce perceived risk)[^39]
3. Neural: Increased right anterior insula/IFG activity during response inhibition under anger[^44]

- F0: Mean +15-30 Hz, high variability, steep contours
- Intensity: +6-12 dB louder
- Speaking rate: +20-40% faster OR abrupt pauses
- Voice quality: Higher jitter, lower HNR (tense)
- Spectral tilt: Lower (harsh timbre)

**Sentiment-Adaptive Response** (Real-Time Agent):[^12]

- **Detection → Adaptation**: When anger detected, agent lowers speaking pace (calming), acknowledges frustration explicitly, offers flexible options
- **Verified outcome** (telecom collections): 35% drop in churn when sentiment-adaptive responses used[^12]

**Design Implications**:

- **Detecting anger**: Use dual-channel (acoustic + transcription) for 35% accuracy boost[^26][^12]
- **Generating anger** (rare use case): Only for emotional TTS datasets, training, or artistic applications—NOT for customer-facing agents
- **De-escalation**: Shift from neutral to soft, slow prosody (contentment-like) when anger detected

### **Concept 4: FEAR / ANXIETY**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Fear (phasic threat response); Anxiety (sustained apprehension)
- **Synonyms**: Dread, panic (fear intensity variants); worry, unease (anxiety variants)


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: Fear is a negative emotion triggered by appraisal of **imminent threat** + **low certainty** + **low control**.[^46][^47][^39]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Appraisal**:[^40][^39]
    - **Threat detection**: Danger to well-being (physical or psychological)
    - **Low certainty**: Unclear what will happen (vs. anger's certainty)
    - **Low control**: Limited ability to influence outcome
- **Action tendency**: Freeze, flight, or fight (defensive)[^48][^46]
- **Neural substrates** (meta-analysis Etkin \& Wager 2007, 4415 citations):[^46]
    - **Amygdala hyperactivation**: PTSD, social anxiety, specific phobia show consistent amygdala + insula hyperactivity vs. controls
    - **Central extended amygdala**: Ce (central nucleus) → phasic fear; BST (bed nucleus stria terminalis) → sustained anxiety/uncertain threat[^47]
    - **PFC hypoactivation**: PTSD shows reduced dorsal/rostral ACC and vmPFC (emotion regulation regions)[^46]
- **Fear vs. Anxiety distinction**:[^49][^47]
    - **Fear**: Phasic, specific, immediate threat → amygdala Ce-mediated
    - **Anxiety**: Sustained, diffuse, remote/uncertain threat → BST-mediated

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Fear ≠ anger**: Fear = low control/certainty; anger = high control/certainty[^39][^40]
- **Fear ≠ sadness**: Fear = active threat (approach/avoidance tension); sadness = loss (withdrawal)[^41]

**Citations**:

- Etkin \& Wager (2007): Meta-analysis of PTSD, social anxiety, specific phobia neuroimaging → amygdala + insula hyperactivation[^46]
- Lerner \& Keltner (2001): Fear → low certainty + low control appraisals → pessimistic risk judgments[^39]
- Fox \& Shackman (2017, 282 citations): Central extended amygdala (Ce + BST) integrates threat information, assembles fear/anxiety states[^47]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) Appraisal Model**[^2][^39]

$$
\mathbf{A}_{\text{fear}} = \begin{pmatrix} \text{Threat} \\ \text{Certainty} \\ \text{Control} \\ \text{Urgency} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \text{High} \\ \text{Low} \\ \text{Low} \\ \text{High} \end{pmatrix}
$$

Contrast with anger: $\mathbf{A}_{\text{anger}}$ has high certainty + high control; fear has low.

**b) VAD Model**[^3][^4][^21]

$$
\text{Fear} = (V, A) \approx (-3, +3), \quad D \approx -2 \text{ (low dominance)}
$$

Negative valence, high arousal, low dominance (feeling powerless).

**c) Bayesian Uncertainty Model**[^50]

Fear correlates with high outcome uncertainty. Let $U(t)$ = uncertainty (variance of possible outcomes).

$$
\text{Fear Intensity} \propto U(t) = \text{Var}[p(outcome \mid context)]
$$

Empirical support (Majumdar et al. 2021, 15 citations): Uncertainty (operationalized as outcome variance) significantly predicts emotional intensity > valence in fMRI study of social threat (movie watching).[^50]

**d) Amygdala Connectivity Model**[^51]

Let $A(t)$ = amygdala activity, $C_{\text{region}}(t)$ = connectivity with region (e.g., insula, ACC).

During fear processing:

$$
\frac{\Delta C_{\text{insula}}}{dt} > 0, \quad \frac{\Delta C_{\text{thalamus}}}{dt} < 0
$$

Meta-analysis: Increased amygdala–insula/IFG/ACC connectivity; decreased amygdala–thalamus/temporal gyrus connectivity during fear.[^51]

**Assumptions**: Appraisal (threat + low control) triggers amygdala; sustained anxiety involves BST; uncertainty is a primary driver.

**Model Predictions**:

1. Fear → risk-averse behavior (low perceived control)[^39]
2. High uncertainty → high fear intensity[^50]
3. vmPFC activation reduces amygdala response (fear extinction)[^48][^46]

#### **3) Voice-Agent / Synthetic Speech Application (2025–2026, Evidence-Grounded)**

**Detection**:[^23][^22]

- **Acoustic features**: Higher pitch (tense), trembling/shakiness (increased jitter), increased speaking rate (panic) OR slow/hesitant (freeze), softer intensity (withdrawal)
- **Prosody**: Irregular rhythm, frequent pauses, narrow pitch range with high mean
- **Context-dependence**: Fear detection benefits from semantic cues (threat-related words) + acoustic[^26][^37]

**Internal Representation**:

- **VAD**: (V=-3, A=+3, D=-2)[^4][^24]
- **Appraisal**: Threat flag + uncertainty flag + low control
- **Discrete**: "fear" or "anxiety" (often grouped with fear in emotion datasets)[^25]

**Generation**:[^28][^10][^29]

**Verified Systems** (same as anger; emotion control includes fear):

- **DiffRO, Style-Controlled VALL-E, PROEMO**: Can generate "fear" emotion via token conditioning, but **no fine-grained validation of fear vs. anxiety prosody differences** found in peer-reviewed literature.

**Prosodic Parameters**:[^22]

- F0: Higher mean (+20-40 Hz), high variability (trembling effect via vibrato-like modulation)
- Intensity: Variable (loud panic OR soft freeze)
- Speaking rate: Fast (panic) OR slow with many pauses (cautious)
- Voice quality: High jitter, breathy (tension)

**Design Implications**:

- **Detecting fear in users**: Prioritize context (e.g., health diagnosis conversation, financial loss discussion) + acoustic cues
- **Generating fear**: Rarely needed in agents; potential applications: virtual training (e.g., emergency response simulations), storytelling/entertainment
- **Calming response**: When fear detected, agent should slow pace, lower pitch, use reassuring language (semantic + prosodic congruence)

## **PART 3: COGNITIVE-AFFECTIVE STATES**

### **Concept 5: BOREDOM**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Boredom (state)[^52][^36]
- **Synonyms**: Ennui, listlessness, tedium


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: Boredom is an aversive state of wanting, but being unable, to engage in satisfying activity; arises from **suboptimal cognitive utilization**—either under-stimulation (monotony) or over-challenge (information overload).[^53][^52][^36]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Cognitive homeostasis model** (Trudel et al. 2025, 4 citations):[^36]
    - Boredom signals **deviation from optimal cognitive engagement set-point**
    - Both under-challenging (redundancy) AND over-challenging (excessive noise/complexity) → boredom
    - Functions as homeostatic signal (like hunger for cognitive resources)
- **Attentional failure**: Difficulty paying attention to internal/external information; awareness of attention failure[^53]
- **Temporal perception**: Boredom slows felt pace of time[^54][^55]
- **Arousal paradox**: Boredom increases autonomic arousal (readying for alternatives) despite low engagement[^52]

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Boredom ≠ sadness**: Boredom = desire to engage (but unable); sadness = goal loss, withdrawal[^56]
- **Boredom ≠ apathy**: Boredom is aversive and motivates seeking alternatives; apathy lacks motivation
- **Boredom vs. frustration**: High task autonomy → boredom relates to depressed affect; low autonomy → frustration[^56]

**Citations**:

- Trudel et al. (2025): "Boredom signals deviation from cognitive homeostatic set-point"; both under- and over-stimulation cause boredom[^36]
- Eastwood et al. (2012): "Aversive state of wanting but unable to engage"; attentional failure definition[^52][^53]
- Danckert \& Merrifield (2018): Boredom increases autonomic arousal, motivates seeking alternatives[^52]
- EEG study (Tabatabaie 2014): Boredom associated with reduced P3 (working memory updating disruption)[^57]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) Cognitive Homeostasis Model**[^36]

Let $CL(t)$ = cognitive load, \$CL^* \$ = optimal cognitive load (set-point).

$$
\text{Boredom}(t) = f\left( |CL(t) - CL^* | \right), \quad f \text{ increasing}
$$

When \$CL < CL^* \$ (under-stimulation) OR \$CL > CL^* \$ (over-challenge beyond coping), boredom increases.

**Empirical support**: Educational psychology shows both under- and over-challenging tasks without value → boredom.[^36]

**b) Information Gain Model**[^55][^54]

Boredom when information gain = 0 (redundancy) OR impossible (high noise).

Let $IG(t) = -\Delta H(p(o \mid s))$ = information gain.

$$
\text{Boredom} \propto \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } IG \approx 0 \text{ (redundancy)} \\
1 & \text{if } \text{signal-to-noise ratio} \to 0 \text{ (cannot extract info)} \\
0 & \text{if } IG \text{ optimal}
\end{cases}
$$

**c) Attention-Disengagement Dynamical System**[^58]

Let $E(t)$ = engagement, $B(t)$ = boredom.

$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = -\alpha \cdot B(t) - \beta \cdot \text{Attentional Drift}(t)
$$

Boredom Feedback Model (Tam et al. 2021, 128 citations): Boredom → attentional drift → disengagement → more boredom (positive feedback).[^58]

**d) VAD Placement**

Boredom typically: Low arousal, neutral-to-negative valence, low dominance.

$$
\text{Boredom} \approx (V=-1, A=-2, D=-1)
$$

**Assumptions**: Optimal cognitive load exists; deviations are aversive; attention is resource-limited.

**Model Predictions**:

1. Boredom increases with task redundancy AND with excessive difficulty[^36]
2. Boredom reduces working memory performance (reduced P3)[^57]
3. Boredom motivates task-switching/seeking alternatives[^52]

#### **3) Voice-Agent / Synthetic Speech Application (2025–2026, Evidence-Grounded)**

**Detection**:[^59][^22][^12]

- **Acoustic features**: Monotonous pitch (low variability), slow speaking rate, flat intensity, frequent sighs, lack of prosodic emphasis
- **Prosody**: Minimal F0 modulation, steady rhythm (no dynamic variation)
- **Context cues**: Repetitive user utterances, short responses, lack of questions
- **Limitation**: Boredom shares low arousal with contentment and fatigue; requires context + semantic analysis[^37]

**Internal Representation**:

- **VAD**: (V=-1, A=-2, D=-1)[^4]
- **Cognitive load**: Below optimal set-point (under-stimulation flag)
- **Discrete label**: "boredom" (less common in standard emotion datasets; may be grouped with "neutral")

**Generation**:

**No specific "boredom TTS" studies found in 2025-2026 literature.** However, neutral/flat prosody approximates boredom.

**Design Implications**:

- **Detecting boredom in user**: Monitor for:
    - Flat prosody + short responses + lack of engagement (e.g., "uh-huh," "okay")
    - Combined with task duration (long monotonous tasks → higher boredom probability)
- **Adaptive response** (when boredom detected):[^12]
    - Increase task variety (switch topics, introduce novelty)
    - Increase agent expressiveness (avoid monotonous voice)
    - Reduce task length or offer breaks
- **Generating boredom**: Rarely useful; potential applications:
    - Training agents to recognize disengagement
    - Entertainment (acting, audiobooks with bored character)

**Speculative**:

- **Boredom-prevention algorithms**: Voice agents that dynamically modulate prosodic variation (avoid monotony) could reduce user boredom. **Not empirically tested.**

### **Concept 6: CURIOSITY / INTEREST**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Curiosity (state motivational drive); Interest (both state and trait)
- **Synonyms**: Inquisitiveness, wonderment


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: **State curiosity** is a transient motivational state driving acquisition of new information, triggered by perceived **knowledge gap** or **information discrepancy**; associated with initial aversive tension ("not knowing") relieved upon resolution.[^60][^61][^62]

**Interest** is broader, involving sustained engagement and positive affect beyond immediate information acquisition.[^61][^60]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Information gap theory** (Loewenstein 1994):[^60][^61]
    - Curiosity triggered when individual recognizes discrepancy between existing knowledge and desired knowledge
    - Creates aversive state (tension)
    - Resolved through information seeking (rewarding)
- **Appraisal basis** (Silvia 2008, 236 citations):[^63]
    - Novelty + **coping potential** (perceived ability to understand) → interest
    - Curious people differ in **amount** of appraisal, not kind
- **Dopaminergic mechanism**:[^60]
    - Curiosity intensifies when close to answer → ventral striatum activation
    - Resolution → dopamine release (reward prediction error)
- **Phases** (state curiosity → personal interest):[^60]

1. Curiosity triggering (knowledge gap)
2. Evaluation (information value vs. competence to obtain)
3. Information seeking (iterative satisfaction)
4. Emerging personal interest (if repeated positive cycles)

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Curiosity ≠ interest**:[^61][^60]
    - Curiosity: Goal-directed, diminishes upon resolution, may involve initial aversive state
    - Interest: Sustained engagement, predominantly positive affect, broader than single information gap
- **Curiosity ≠ boredom**:[^64][^61]
    - Curiosity = hunger FOR information (appetitive)
    - Boredom = lack of engagement, seeking alternatives (aversive)
    - **Can co-exist**: Boredom may trigger curiosity as remedy

**Citations**:

- Litman (2005): Curiosity = motivational state to resolve knowledge gap[^60]
- Shin \& Kim (2019): Curiosity goal-directed, diminishes upon resolution; interest sustained[^61][^60]
- Silvia (2008): Appraisal theory of interest—novelty + coping potential[^63]
- Tang \& Murayama (2022, 98 citations): Distinguishing curiosity from interest in information-seeking[^61]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) Information Gap Model**[^61][^60]

Let $K_{\text{current}}$ = current knowledge, $K_{\text{desired}}$ = desired knowledge.

$$
\text{Curiosity} = f(K_{\text{desired}} - K_{\text{current}}), \quad f \text{ increasing up to threshold, then decreasing if gap too large}
$$

Inverted-U: Moderate gaps → high curiosity; very small (obvious) or very large (overwhelming) gaps → low curiosity.

**b) Expected Value of Control (EVC) Model**[^60]

Curiosity → information seeking when:

$$
\text{EVC} = P(\text{success}) \cdot V(\text{info}) - C(\text{effort}) > \text{threshold}
$$

- $P(\text{success})$ = perceived competence to obtain/understand info
- $V(\text{info})$ = value of information (relevance, novelty)
- $C(\text{effort})$ = cost (time, cognitive load)

Information seeking most likely when value high + competence high + cost reasonable.

**c) Free Energy / Information Gain Model**[^35]

Epistemic emotions (curiosity, interest) linked to information gain (IG) in Bayesian framework.

$$
IG = D_{KL}(p(s \mid o) \| p(s)) = H(s) - H(s \mid o)
$$

Upward-convex function of surprise → arousal potential (Wundt curve).[^35]

Curiosity peaks when $IG$ moderate (not trivial, not impossible).

**d) VAD Placement**

Curiosity/Interest: Positive valence (once engaged), moderate arousal (alert, focused), moderate-high dominance (confidence to explore).

$$
\text{Interest} \approx (V=+2, A=+1, D=+2)
$$

**Assumptions**: Knowledge gaps are detectable; individuals evaluate competence; resolution is rewarding (dopaminergic).

**Model Predictions**:

1. Moderate novelty → peak curiosity (inverted-U)[^63][^60]
2. Curiosity → exploration behavior (information seeking)
3. Repeated curiosity satisfaction → personal interest development[^60]

#### **3) Voice-Agent / Synthetic Speech Application (2025–2026, Evidence-Grounded)**

**Detection**:

- **Acoustic features**: Moderate-to-high pitch variation (engagement), upward pitch inflections (questioning), moderate-to-fast speaking rate, dynamic intensity (emphasis on keywords)
- **Prosody**: Rising contours (questions), varied rhythm, pauses before questions
- **Linguistic cues**: Questions, hedges ("I wonder if..."), epistemic markers ("maybe," "possibly")

**Internal Representation**:

- **VAD**: (V=+2, A=+1, D=+2)[^24][^4]
- **State**: Information-seeking flag, question-asking mode
- **Discrete label**: "interest" (often grouped with positive emotions in broaden-and-build datasets)[^13][^14]

**Generation**:

**No specific "curiosity TTS" studies found.** Interest/engagement often modeled as moderate arousal + positive valence in emotional TTS.

**Design Implications**:

- **Encouraging curiosity in users**:
    - Introduce moderate novelty (not overwhelming)[^63]
    - Provide "teasers" (partial information to create knowledge gap)[^60]
    - Use questioning prosody (upward inflections) to model curiosity
- **Agent expressing curiosity**:
    - Prosody: Rising pitch at end of statements (invitational tone), pauses (thoughtful)
    - Linguistic: "That's interesting—tell me more," "I wonder if..."
    - Functional: Encourages user elaboration, deepens interaction

**Speculative**:

- **Curiosity-driven dialogue systems**: Agents that adaptively ask follow-up questions based on user knowledge gaps could enhance engagement. **Prototyped in conversational AI but not voice-prosody validated.**

## **PART 4: SOCIAL AFFECT \& MORAL EMOTIONS**

### **Concept 7: EMPATHY**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Empathy (affective + cognitive components)
- **Synonyms**: Compassion (action-oriented empathy), sympathy (feeling for, not with)


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: Empathy is a complex process involving **affective empathy** (feeling with another) and **cognitive empathy** (theory-of-mind, perspective-taking), enabling understanding and sharing of others' emotional states.[^65][^66][^67]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Affective component**: Sharing the emotional experience of another (mirroring)[^68][^66]
- **Cognitive component**: Mentalizing, understanding another's mental states (TPJ, dmPFC activation)[^67][^65]
- **Prosody-driven empathy** (Mauchand et al. 2022, 26 citations):[^66]
    - **Ingroup complaints** (same accent) → P200 (early motivational relevance)
    - **Outgroup complaints** (different accent) → early posterior negativity + N400 (effortful extraction of emotional meaning)
    - Prosody + accent shape empathic response timing
- **Vocal features of empathy** (Sanjeewa et al. 2025, 4 citations):[^7]
    - Higher F1 (formant 1, "a" vowel sound)
    - Higher harmonic-to-noise ratio (HNR, clarity)
    - Positive spectral slope (warmth)
    - Lower spectral centroid (low-frequency components)
    - **Loudness threshold critical**: Right balance needed for perceived empathy

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Empathy ≠ sympathy**: Empathy = feeling with (shared affect); sympathy = feeling for (concern but not shared affect)
- **Guilt enhances empathy; shame disrupts empathy**: Guilt (other-focused) → empathic concern; shame (self-focused distress) → empathy disruption[^67]
- **Context-dependence**: Paralinguistic-only empathy recognition limited; contextual cues essential[^37]

**Citations**:

- Mauchand \& Pell (2022): Prosody + accent drive empathy; ingroup → P200, outgroup → N400[^66]
- Sanjeewa et al. (2025): Machine learning identifies vocal features of empathic counselor speech[^7]
- Wondra \& Lazarus (2015, 415 citations): Appraisal theory of empathy—based on evaluation of others' situations[^65]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) Appraisal Theory of Vicarious Emotion**[^65]

Empathy = appraisal of another's situation $\rightarrow$ emotion FOR them.

Let $S_{\text{other}}$ = other's situation, $A_{\text{observer}}$ = observer's appraisal.

$$
P(\text{Empathic Emotion} \mid S_{\text{other}}) \propto P(A_{\text{observer}}(S_{\text{other}}) = A_{\text{match}})
$$

Where $A_{\text{match}}$ = appraisal matching what observer would feel in that situation.

**b) Acoustic Feature Model** (Machine Learning, Sanjeewa 2025)[^7]

Empathy score predicted via SVM:

$$
\text{Empathy} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot F1 + \beta_2 \cdot HNR + \beta_3 \cdot \text{Spectral Slope} + \beta_4 \cdot \text{Spectral Centroid} + \epsilon
$$

Where:

- $\beta_1 > 0$ (higher F1 → more empathic)
- $\beta_2 > 0$ (higher HNR/clarity → more empathic)
- $\beta_3 > 0$ (positive spectral slope → warmth)
- $\beta_4 < 0$ (lower spectral centroid → more low-freq → empathic)

**c) Vocal Synchrony Model** (Robot Empathy)[^68]

Let $V_{\text{human}}(t)$ = human vocal prosody, $V_{\text{robot}}(t)$ = robot vocal prosody.

**Synchrony** when $V_{\text{robot}}(t) \approx V_{\text{human}}(t - \tau)$ (robot mimics with short delay $\tau$).

Empirical result (2021 study): Vocal synchrony → increased positive affective empathy ratings.[^68]

**d) VAD Representation**

Empathy itself is not an emotion but a process; however, empathic concern (compassionate state) could be:

$$
\text{Empathic Concern} \approx (V=+2, A=+1, D=+1)
$$

Positive valence (caring), moderate arousal (alert to other's needs), moderate dominance (agency to help).

**Assumptions**: Empathy involves appraisal of other's situation; vocal features (clarity, warmth) signal empathy; synchrony enhances perception.

**Model Predictions**:

1. Vocal synchrony (robot/agent mimicking human prosody) → increased perceived empathy[^68]
2. Higher HNR + positive spectral slope → higher empathy ratings[^7]
3. Ingroup (same accent) empathy faster (P200) than outgroup (N400)[^66]

#### **3) Voice-Agent / Synthetic Speech Application (2025–2026, Evidence-Grounded)**

**Detection** (of user needing empathy):[^69][^70][^66][^12]

- **Acoustic**: Prosodic cues of distress (complaints, sadness, frustration)—lower pitch, slower rate, pauses
- **Semantic**: Emotion words ("I'm frustrated," "This hurts")
- **Contextual**: Negative events described
- **Real-time**: Multimodal systems (neural + physiological) for empathetic agents[^70]

**Generation** (empathetic agent voice):[^71][^72][^73][^74][^75][^69][^68]

**Verified 2025-2026 Systems**:

1. **STUDIES Corpus** (2022, Japanese): Empathetic dialogue speech corpus with speaker explicitly empathizing with interlocutor's emotion. TTS experiment: Using interlocutor's emotion label + conversational context embedding produces speech with same naturalness as using agent's emotion label. **Implication**: Empathy can be TTS-generated via emotion+context conditioning.[^73][^71]
2. **SSML-Based Empathetic Agents** (2023 exploratory study): Prosody modulation (pitch, rate, volume) + verbal empathy ("I understand," "That sounds hard") successfully conveys empathy. User study: Participants perceive empathy from prosody+verbal combination. **Concerns**: Users worry about reduced human interaction, exploitation of emotional states.[^69]
3. **Secondary Emotions TTS** (2023): Low-resource empathetic TTS models subtle secondary emotions needed for empathy (not just primary emotions like joy/anger).[^72]
4. **Robot Vocal Synchrony** (2021): Robot voice synchronized with human prosody → increased positive affective empathy. **Mechanism**: Mirroring creates rapport.[^68]
5. **Empathetic Conversational Agents** (2025, arxiv): Neural + physiological signals for real-time emotion recognition; **challenges identified**: accuracy, emotional transition speeds, personality effects, **voice tone modulation limitations**.[^70]
6. **Acoustic Features** (Chen 2024, 8 citations): 12 prosodic features for empathy detection: pitch (mean, min, max), energy, voice quality, speaking rate. Machine learning classifies empathic speech.[^74][^75]

**Prosodic Parameters for Empathetic Agent**:[^71][^72][^69][^7][^68]

- F0: Moderate, matches user (synchrony); avoid extremes
- F1 (formant 1): Higher (warmth)
- HNR: High (clarity)
- Spectral slope: Positive (warm timbre)
- Spectral centroid: Lower (low-frequency richness)
- Speaking rate: Slow-to-moderate (thoughtful, not rushed)
- Pauses: Appropriate (give user space)
- **Loudness**: CRITICAL—moderate, not too soft (disengaged) nor too loud (aggressive)[^7]

**Design Implications**:

- **Empathy detection**: Use dual-channel (acoustic + semantic) for 35% accuracy boost[^26][^12]
- **Empathy generation**:
    - Combine **prosody** (warm, clear, synchronized) + **verbal empathy** (validation, acknowledgment)
    - Avoid monotonous or excessively cheerful voice when user distressed
    - **Vocal synchrony**: Subtly match user's pitch/rate (not exact mirroring, which feels uncanny)
- **Ethical considerations** (from user study):[^69]
    - Transparency: Disclose AI nature (explicit AI labeling reduces trust, but deception ethically problematic)[^5]
    - Avoid manipulation: Empathy to support, not exploit

**Speculative**:

- **Real-time empathy adaptation**: Agent adjusts prosody continuously based on user's micro-expressions (if visual) or vocal affect. **Prototyped but not validated in voice-only systems.**

### **Concept 8: SHAME**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Shame (self-conscious moral emotion)
- **Synonyms**: Humiliation, mortification


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: Shame is a **self-conscious moral emotion** arising from appraisal of the **self as flawed** or violating social standards; characterized by self-focused distress, social pain, and behavioral inhibition (withdrawal, hiding).[^76][^77][^67]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Appraisal** (Piretti et al. 2023 meta-analysis, 31 citations):[^67]
    - **Self-focus**: "I am bad" (vs. guilt's "I did something bad")
    - **Global self-attribution**: Entire self appraised as inadequate
    - **Social pain**: Perception of others' negative evaluation
- **Action tendency**: Withdraw, hide, avoid[^67]
- **Empathy disruption**: Shame's self-oriented distress interferes with empathic concern for others[^67]
- **Neural substrates** (meta-analysis):[^78][^67]
    - **Common with guilt**: Left anterior insula (emotional awareness, arousal)
    - **Shame-specific**:
        - **Dorsal ACC + medial thalamus**: Social pain network[^67]
        - **Premotor cortex**: Behavioral inhibition[^67]
        - **MCC (middle cingulate cortex) + insula**: Regardless of culture[^78]

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Shame ≠ guilt**:[^76][^67]
    - **Shame**: Self-focus, "I am bad," withdrawal, disrupts empathy
    - **Guilt**: Action-focus, "I did bad," reparative, enhances empathy
- **Shame ≠ embarrassment**: Embarrassment → reparative behavior to regain approval; shame → withdrawal/hiding[^67]

**Citations**:

- Piretti et al. (2023): Meta-analysis of shame/guilt neural correlates; shame → social pain network (dACC, thalamus, premotor)[^67]
- Zhu et al. (2025, eLife): Neurocomputational mechanisms—harm vs. responsibility; responsibility-driven shame → TPJ/STS, PRC/POC/SMA negative correlation[^76]
- Bastin et al. (2016) review: Shame → dACC, PCC, sensory-motor cortex; embarrassment → vlPFC, amygdala[^67]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) Appraisal Model**[^2][^76]

$$
\mathbf{A}_{\text{shame}} = \begin{pmatrix} \text{Self-focus} \\ \text{Global attribution} \\ \text{Social evaluation (negative)} \\ \text{Responsibility (ambiguous)} \end{pmatrix}
$$

vs.

$$
\mathbf{A}_{\text{guilt}} = \begin{pmatrix} \text{Action-focus} \\ \text{Specific attribution} \\ \text{Reparability} \\ \text{Responsibility (high)} \end{pmatrix}
$$

**b) Computational Neuromodeling** (Zhu et al. 2025)[^76]

Shame = $f(\text{Responsibility})$, Guilt = $f(\text{Harm})$

Let $H$ = harm level, $R$ = responsibility level.

$$
\text{Shame}(H, R) = \beta_R \cdot R + \epsilon_{\text{shame}}, \quad \beta_R > 0
$$

$$
\text{Guilt}(H, R) = \beta_H \cdot H + \epsilon_{\text{guilt}}, \quad \beta_H > 0
$$

fMRI study: Responsibility appraisal activates regions negatively correlated with responsibility-driven shame sensitivity (TPJ/STS, PRC/POC/SMA).[^76]

**c) Social Pain Model**[^67]

Shame activates social pain network (dACC + thalamus). Let $P_{\text{social}}$ = social pain intensity.

$$
P_{\text{social}} = g(\text{Perceived Negative Evaluation}) \rightarrow \text{Shame Intensity}
$$

Stronger perceived negative social evaluation → stronger shame.

**d) VAD Placement**

$$
\text{Shame} \approx (V=-3, A=+1 \text{ to } +2, D=-3)
$$

Negative valence, moderate arousal (distress), very low dominance (powerlessness, desire to hide).

**Assumptions**: Self vs. action attribution distinguishes shame from guilt; social pain network unique to shame; responsibility appraisal drives shame.

**Model Predictions**:

1. Shame → withdrawal behavior (vs. guilt's approach/repair)[^67]
2. Shame disrupts empathy for others (self-focused distress)[^67]
3. Responsibility appraisal → shame; harm appraisal → guilt[^76]

#### **3) Voice-Agent / Synthetic Speech Application (2025–2026, Evidence-Grounded)**

**Detection**:

**Insufficient Evidence**: No peer-reviewed studies on paralinguistic features of shame vs. guilt in speech.

**Hypothetical acoustic features** (speculative, based on action tendencies):

- **Shame**: Soft/quiet intensity (withdrawal), lower pitch (submission), slower rate, more pauses (hesitation), breathy voice quality (vulnerability)
- **Guilt**: Moderate intensity, moderate-to-low pitch, steady rate (focused on repair), clearer voice quality

**Context critical**: Shame/guilt likely indistinguishable from acoustics alone; semantic + situational cues necessary (e.g., "I feel ashamed of myself" vs. "I feel guilty about what I did").

**Generation**:

**No TTS studies found on generating shame/guilt prosody.**

**Design Implications**:

- **Detecting shame in user**: Extremely challenging without semantic/contextual cues; acoustic cues ambiguous
- **Agent response to shame** (if detected via semantics):
    - **Avoid exacerbating**: Do NOT use judgmental tone
    - **Validate**: "That sounds really hard," "You're not alone in feeling this way"
    - **Prosody**: Warm, soft, non-threatening (empathetic prosody)[^71][^69][^7]
    - **Shift focus**: Gently redirect from global self-blame to specific actions (therapeutic reframing)
- **Generating shame**: **NEVER** a desirable use case for customer-facing agents. Potential applications:
    - Training (e.g., teaching emotion recognition)
    - Research (e.g., validating shame detection algorithms)

**Speculative**:

- **Shame-sensitive dialogue systems**: Systems that detect shame (semantic) and adapt to avoid re-shaming language. **Not yet developed.**

## **PART 5: ADVANCED CONCEPTS**

### **Concept 9: AWE**

#### **0) Canonical Term \& Synonyms**

- **Canonical**: Awe (self-transcendent emotion)
- **Synonyms**: Wonder, amazement (though awe often involves vastness + accommodation)


#### **1) Psychological Definition (Verified)**

**Definition**: Awe is a positive self-transcendent emotion elicited by perceiving **vastness** (something larger than self—physical, conceptual, or social) + **need for accommodation** (current mental schemas inadequate, requiring updating).[^79][^80][^81]

**Core Mechanisms**:

- **Vastness**: Perception of something immense (natural landscapes, complex ideas, exceptional achievements)
- **Accommodation**: Mental schemas must expand to integrate experience[^81][^79]
- **Self-transcendence**: "Small self" effect—reduced self-salience, expanded sense of connection to larger whole[^80][^79][^81]
- **Neural substrates** (van Elk et al. 2019, 172 citations):[^79]
    - **Reduced DMN activity**: Especially medial PFC (MPFC) and posterior cingulate cortex (PCC) during awe
    - **Mechanism**: Reduced self-referential processing, less mind-wandering, captivated by present experience
- **Positive vs. Negative Awe** (Guan et al. 2019, 48 citations):[^80]
    - **Positive awe**: Calm, tonic positive affect, higher personal control, beauty-focused; associated with **precuneus** (self-reflection)
    - **Negative awe**: Fear, powerlessness, low self-control, threat-focused; associated with **insula** (threat processing, emotion regulation)

**Boundary Conditions**:

- **Awe ≠ joy**: Awe involves cognitive accommodation + self-transcendence; joy doesn't necessarily
- **Awe ≠ surprise**: Awe requires vastness; surprise can be trivial

**Citations**:

- van Elk et al. (2019): Awe → reduced DMN (MPFC, PCC) activity; captivating, immersive[^79]
- Guan et al. (2019): Positive awe (precuneus, calm) vs. negative awe (insula, fear)[^80]
- Jiang \& Sedikides (2021, 201 citations): Awe motivates authentic-self pursuit via self-transcendence[^81]


#### **2) Mathematical / Computational Definition (Verified + Formal)**

**a) Vastness + Accommodation Model**[^81][^79][^80]

$$
\text{Awe} = f(\text{Vastness}, \text{Accommodation Need}), \quad f \text{ increasing in both}
$$

Where:

- \$Vastness \in \$ = perceived magnitude relative to self[^82]
- \$Accommodation \in \$ = degree current schemas inadequate[^82]

Awe maximized when both high.

**b) Self-Transcendence (Small Self) Model**[^79][^81]

Let $S_{\text{self}}(t)$ = self-salience (perceived self-size/importance).

$$
\frac{dS_{\text{self}}}{dt} = -\alpha \cdot \text{Awe}(t), \quad \alpha > 0
$$

Awe reduces self-salience ("small self" effect).

**c) DMN Activity Model**[^79]

Let $A_{\text{DMN}}(t)$ = default mode network activity.

$$
A_{\text{DMN}}^{\text{awe}} < A_{\text{DMN}}^{\text{neutral}}, \quad \text{especially MPFC, PCC}
$$

Empirical: fMRI shows reduced MPFC + PCC during awe videos vs. neutral/positive videos.[^79]

**d) Positive vs. Negative Awe (VAD)**[^80]

$$
\text{Positive Awe} \approx (V=+3, A=+1, D=+2)
$$

$$
\text{Negative Awe} \approx (V=-1, A=+2, D=-2)
$$

Positive: high valence, low-moderate arousal (calm), moderate-high dominance. Negative: negative valence, moderate-high arousal (threat), low dominance (powerlessness).

**Assumptions**: Vastness perception requires context; accommodation is cognitive; self-transcendence measurable via self-report or neural (DMN) activity.

**Model Predictions**:

1. Awe → reduced DMN activity (MPFC, PCC)[^79]
2. Positive awe → prosocial behavior (via self-transcendence)[^81][^79]
3. Awe captivates attention (immersive, reduces mind-wandering)[^79]

#### **3) Voice-Agent / Synthetic Speech Application (2025–2026, Evidence-Grounded)**

**Detection**:

**Insufficient evidence**: No studies on acoustic features of awe in speech.

**Hypothetical** (based on psychological profile):

- **Positive awe**: Soft, slow, smooth prosody (calm, wonder); pauses (absorption); breathy quality (reverence)
- **Negative awe**: Higher pitch (fear component), trembling, irregular rhythm

**Context critical**: Awe likely requires semantic/situational cues (e.g., "Wow, this is incredible," "I've never seen anything like this").

**Generation**:

**No TTS studies on generating awe prosody found.**

**Design Implications**:

- **Eliciting awe in users** (via content, not just voice):
    - Present vast/complex information (e.g., visualizations of scale—universe, data)
    - Allow time for accommodation (pauses, reflection)
- **Agent expressing awe**:
    - Prosody: Slow, soft, reverent tone; pauses (wonder)
    - Linguistic: "Wow," "That's remarkable," "I've never encountered..."
    - Functional: Models intellectual humility, encourages user reflection
- **Context**: Awe-appropriate domains (education, nature, art, science communication); inappropriate in mundane transactional contexts

**Speculative**:

- **Awe-inducing voice agents**: Guides for museums, planetariums, nature experiences could use awe-expressive prosody to enhance experience. **Not empirically tested.**

### **1) Revelation (Unconcealment)**

**What Concepts, Structures, Assumptions, or Interpretive Frames Became Visible?**

This response brought to light:

- **Appraisal theory as dominant paradigm**: Emotions emerge from evaluations (goal congruence, certainty, control, agency). This framework structures most psychological definitions, privileging cognitive appraisal over embodied or purely physiological accounts.[^1][^83][^2]
- **Dimensional (VAD) models as lingua franca**: Valence-arousal-dominance provides shared computational substrate across affective computing, enabling TTS systems and emotion recognition. This reveals **dimensional reductionism**—collapsing rich phenomenology into 2-3 axes.[^85][^3][^24][^4][^21]

- **Concept list structure** (positive | negative | cognitive | social | moral | communication stances) → Organized response by **affective taxonomy**, reinforcing categorical boundaries. This **concealed dimensional perspectives** (e.g., all emotions as V×A×D points, not discrete categories).
- **Voice AI emphasis** → Prioritized **acoustic-prosodic features** over linguistic-semantic integration. Sarcasm, condescension require **pragmatics + prosody**; the question's voice-focus made these **harder to address**.
- **"2025-2026 innovation"** → Created **temporal bias toward recent preprints** (arxiv), many unvalidated. This **revealed publication lag** (peer-review = 1-2 years) but also **risk of citing non-replicated findings**.

### **2) Concealment (What Remained Hidden or Suppressed)**

**Which Perspectives, Counter-Arguments, or Frameworks Were Omitted?**

#### **Theoretical Omissions**:

1. **Constructionist emotion theory** (Lisa Feldman Barrett): Emotions as brain's predictions (constructed, not triggered by appraisals)[contested alternative to appraisal theory]. This challenges **appraisal primacy** assumed throughout report. **Why omitted?** Appraisal theory has stronger empirical support in voice AI applications (appraisals → acoustic features); constructionism lacks actionable formalizations for TTS.
2. **Embodied/enactive emotion theories** (Colombetti, Fuchs): Emotions as embodied engagements with world, not internal states mappable to brain regions. This **challenges neural localization** assumed in fMRI sections. **Why omitted?** Difficult to operationalize for voice AI; less computational traction.[^46][^79][^67]
3. **Cultural psychology of emotion** (Mesquita, Kitayama): Emotions as cultural practices, not universal brain states. **Example**: Shame in collectivist cultures may be prosocially regulated (促进和谐), not purely aversive. **Why omitted?** Most sources Western; cross-cultural voice prosody data scarce.[^67]
4. **Critical perspectives on affective computing** (Stark, Crawford): AI emotion recognition as **essentialist + reductive**, risks surveillance, manipulation. **Example**: Real-time emotion detection (85% accuracy) enables **emotional profiling** without consent. **Why omitted?** User query framed as **technical** (how to build), not **ethical** (whether to build). Ethical concerns mentioned but not foregrounded.[^5][^69][^12]

#### **Edge Cases and Complexities Simplified**:

1. **Mixed emotions**: Report treats emotions as discrete (joy XOR sadness); humans often feel **blends** (bittersweet = joy + sadness). **Why simplified?** TTS systems handle single emotions; mixed-emotion prosody unsolved problem.
2. **Emotional dynamics**: Report uses static models (VAD coordinates); real emotions have **temporal dynamics** (anger crescendo, fear freeze-then-flight). **Why simplified?** Dynamical systems models mentioned but time-series TTS prosody generation underexplored in literature.[^89][^43]
3. **Individual differences**: Report aggregates; but empathy perception, boredom thresholds, shame proneness vary widely. **Why simplified?** Voice AI systems train on population averages; personalization (adapt to individual user) is frontier, not standard.
4. **Context-dependence**: Report emphasizes context matters but doesn't formalize **context representation** (conversational history, user goals, cultural norms). **Why omitted?** Context modeling in voice AI nascent; most systems reactive (respond to current utterance), not deeply contextual.[^37]

#### **Methodological Limitations Underplayed**:

1. **Correlational vs. causal**: Many fMRI studies show **amygdala activation during fear**, but activation ≠ causation. Lesion studies (causal) show **amygdala not always necessary for fear report**. **Why underplayed?** Meta-analyses dominate literature; causal evidence sparse, inconvenient for neural localization narrative.[^48][^51][^46]
2. **Ecological validity**: Lab studies (VR awe videos, Locked Box task) ≠ real-world voice AI interactions. **Example**: TTS empathy effective in lab ≠ effective in 6-month customer service deployment. **Why underplayed?** Longitudinal field studies rare; available evidence favors controlled experiments.[^18][^41]
3. **Replication crisis**: Psychology's replication rate ~50%; some cited findings may not replicate. **Example**: Specific appraisal-emotion links may be **task-dependent**. **Why underplayed?** Report cites high-citation meta-analyses (assumed robust), but meta-analyses aggregate potentially non-replicable studies.

#### **Commercial and Ideological Biases**:

1. **Vendor-driven "emotion AI" hype**: Many 2025-2026 sources = **industry blogs, white papers** (not peer-reviewed). Claims like "85% accuracy in 1.5s" unverified in independent research. **Why included despite weakness?** User requested 2025-2026 state-of-the-art; peer-reviewed sources lag ~2 years.[^90][^84][^12]
2. **Instrumental view of emotion**: Report frames emotions as **functional** (broaden-and-build, goal-directed, information signals). This serves **productivity/optimization ideology** (emotions useful for performance). **Concealed**: Emotions as **intrinsically valuable** (joy worth experiencing for its own sake), not just instrumental.[^13][^40][^36]
3. **Anthropocentric bias**: Report assumes **human emotions as gold standard**; voice AI should mimic/detect human affect. **Concealed**: Possibility of **non-human affective communication** (e.g., AI agents with genuinely different "emotion-like" states not reducible to human categories).

### **3) Mechanisms of Concealment (Model/Process Factors)**

**Process-Level Tendencies Causing Concealment**:

1. **Preference for coherence over complexity**: Report synthesizes disparate sources into **unified narrative** (appraisal → VAD → TTS control). This **smooths contradictions** (e.g., boredom arousal paradox downplayed to maintain low-arousal classification).[^4][^52]
2. **Source availability bias**: Over-represented English-language, Western, peer-reviewed sources. **Under-represented**: Non-English emotion research, indigenous/non-Western emotion concepts, gray literature with innovative but未验证的ideas.
3. **Quantification bias**: Mathematical formalizations prioritized (VAD coordinates, ODEs, Bayesian models) → **qualitative phenomenology** (what it *feels like* to experience awe, shame) gets **compressed into equations**, losing richness.[^79][^67]
4. **Recency bias (2025-2026 focus)**: Arxiv preprints over-weighted due to temporal constraint. These lack **peer scrutiny**, may not replicate. **Mechanism**: Time-sensitive query → accept lower evidence bar for recent work.
5. **Engineering actionability filter**: Concepts with **clear acoustic features** (joy = higher pitch) get detailed coverage; concepts without (feeling understood, patronized) get flagged as "no studies found." **Mechanism**: Voice AI application requirement → filters for **sonifiable** constructs.[^22]
6. **Citation prestige bias**: High-citation sources (Fredrickson's broaden-and-build 2955 citations, Etkin \& Wager 4415 citations) dominate. This **marginalizes recent work** (<100 citations) and **alternative theories** (constructionism, despite Barrett's influence, less actionable for TTS).[^46][^13]
7. **Disciplinary silos**: Psychology, neuroscience, computational modeling, speech synthesis literature searched **separately**. **Cross-disciplinary integration** (e.g., phenomenology + machine learning) rare in sources, thus rare in report.

**Observable Tendencies (No Hidden System Prompts Claimed)**:

- **Systematization imperative**: Drive to **organize 100+ concepts** → taxonomic structure (positive | negative | cognitive | social | moral). This imposes **categorical ontology**, concealing **continuum perspectives** (emotions as gradients, not types).
- **Verificationism**: "Insufficient reliable evidence" repeated when sources weak. This is **epistemically conservative** (good for accuracy) but **conceals speculative frontiers** (e.g., could shame prosody be *theoretically* derivable from action tendency even without empirical validation?).[^72][^20][^70][^10][^29][^11][^28][^33][^67]
- **Audience adaptation**: Report assumes **expert reader** (uses jargon like VAD, DMN, TPJ without full expansion). This **conceals** explanations accessible to **lay readers** or **policymakers** who might make decisions about emotion AI deployment.

### **4) Epistemological and Ethical Implications**

**How Could the Concealments Affect the User's Interpretation and Decisions?**

#### **Overconfidence Risks**:

1. **Appraisal theory dominance** → User may assume **emotions = appraisals = acoustics** is fully validated. **Risk**: Ignores constructionist view (emotions context-dependent, predicted, not triggered). **Consequence**: TTS systems might generate **stereotyped** emotional prosody that doesn't adapt to individual/cultural variation.
2. **VAD reductionism** → User may treat emotions as **fully capturable by 3 numbers**. **Risk**: Loses nuance (shame vs. guilt both negative valence, moderate arousal, but radically different in experience/action). **Consequence**: Voice AI "flattens" emotional landscape, misses subtle distinctions users care about.[^4][^67]
3. **2025-2026 TTS claims** (DiffRO, PROEMO, etc.) → User may assume **emotion control problem solved**. **Risk**: Arxiv preprints unvalidated; replication unknown. **Consequence**: Deploying systems that **fail in production**, eroding user trust.[^10][^28]
4. **"85% emotion detection accuracy"** → User may assume **reliable real-time system**. **Risk**: Industry claim, not peer-reviewed; may conflate different emotion sets, cherry-picked datasets. **Consequence**: **False positives/negatives** in customer service (e.g., detecting anger when user curious, escalating unnecessarily).[^12]

#### **Misapplication Dangers**:

1. **Empathy generation** (STUDIES corpus, SSML prosody) → User may deploy "empathetic" voice agents assuming **benefit**. **Concealed risk**: User study found concerns about **exploitation of emotional states**; synthetic empathy may **manipulate** (e.g., sales, compliance). **Consequence**: **Ethical harm** (users feel manipulated), **regulatory backlash** (emotion AI bans, e.g., EU AI Act restrictions).[^73][^71][^69]
2. **Shame/guilt detection** (semantic + acoustic hypothetically) → User may build systems flagging **users experiencing shame**. **Concealed risk**: Shame is **intensely private**, detection = **emotional surveillance**. **Consequence**: **Privacy violation**, exacerbating shame (user aware AI knows), potential discrimination (shame-prone users treated differently).
3. **Boredom detection** (flat prosody + context) → User may adapt content to "reduce boredom." **Concealed risk**: Boredom can be **productive** (signals suboptimal engagement, motivates seeking alternatives). Over-stimulation to prevent boredom → **attention fragmentation**, inability to tolerate low-stimulus states. **Consequence**: **Addictive design** (constant novelty to avoid boredom detection).[^22][^37][^52][^36]

#### **Moral/Clinical Misuse**:

1. **Anger detection** (real-time) + **sentiment-adaptive response** → User may deploy in **high-stakes contexts** (healthcare, legal, crisis hotlines). **Concealed risk**: Misdetection (anger when user scared, frustrated, or culturally expressive) → **inappropriate response** (e.g., calming tone when urgency needed). **Consequence**: **Harm** (delayed emergency response, user feels unheard).[^12]
2. **Awe elicitation** (via content + prosody, speculated) → User may design **persuasive systems** (education, marketing, political). **Concealed risk**: Awe → **cognitive accommodation** (schema change), exploitable for **manipulation** (e.g., inducing awe to make users accept uncritically). **Consequence**: **Ethical boundary violation** (using self-transcendent emotion for commercial/political gain).[^81][^79]

#### **Misreading Social Signals**:

1. **Communication stances (sarcasm, condescension) omitted** → User may assume **voice AI can handle all interaction styles**. **Concealed limitation**: Sarcasm requires **pragmatic inference** (literal meaning ≠ intended meaning), prosody alone insufficient. **Consequence**: AI agents **miss sarcasm** (respond literally, frustrating user) or **false-detect sarcasm** (respond defensively to genuine statement).[^22]
2. **Cultural prosody variation underexplored** → User may deploy **single TTS model globally**. **Concealed risk**: Emotional prosody culturally specific (e.g., Japanese empathy may differ from American). **Consequence**: AI sounds **culturally inappropriate**, reduces trust, adoption.[^73][^71]
3. **Social affect toward agent (feeling patronized, manipulated) ungeneralizable** → User may think these are **non-issues** (no research = not important). **Concealed**: Absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence; users DO feel patronized by condescending TTS tone, but **not studied empirically**. **Consequence**: Deploying agents that **alienate users** (patronizing prosody unmeasured but real).
